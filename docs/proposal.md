# Proposal: Construction choices and intervention transfer in sycophancy model organisms

Oct 7, 2026 · @Anthony

## TL;DR

We test whether two cheap construction knobs, LoRA rank and benign-data
fraction, determine whether interventions on sycophancy model organisms
(MOs) transfer to the natural sycophancy of the instruct model they were
built from. We train a 3 × 3 grid of LoRA MOs, measure each model's
trait properties (trait rate, linear decodability by layer, leakage,
capability), extract a sycophancy direction from every model, and ablate
each direction in every other model. The output is an n × n transfer
matrix plus a regression of transfer on MO properties: a first
quantitative answer to which MO properties predict faithful intervention
response.

## Motivation

MOs are only useful for testing mitigations if their response to a
mitigation predicts how naturally misaligned models respond. For the
most catastrophic failure modes, the natural model does not exist yet,
so we need to judge an MO's faithfulness from properties we can measure
on the MO itself.

Prior work shows MO results are sensitive to construction details.
Redwood finds LoRA MOs less robust to untargeted training than full
fine-tunes. Sleeper-agent replications flip conclusions with optimizer
and distillation choices. Dubiński et al. (2026) show benign-data
dilution can hide misalignment behind contextual triggers instead of
removing it. No one has measured how these construction choices change
transfer of interventions to a natural model.

This project makes that measurement for one trait, with the construction
knobs varied systematically. It contributes to the group's goal of a
science of MOs and produces a documented set of sycophancy MOs others
can reuse.

## Research questions and hypotheses

**Main question:** do LoRA rank and benign-data fraction control whether
a sycophancy direction extracted from an MO removes the natural
sycophancy of its parent instruct model?

**Sub-questions:**

1.  How do rank and benign fraction change the trait's legibility
    (linear decodability, layer spread)?

2.  Does mechanistic similarity (cosine between trait directions)
    predict intervention transfer?

3.  Which MO properties, measurable without the natural model, predict
    transfer to it?

**Hypotheses (each falsifiable):**

- **H1, legibility:** lower rank and lower benign fraction give a more
  linearly decodable trait concentrated in fewer layers.

- **H2, mechanism predicts transfer:** transfer from model i to model j
  increases with the cosine similarity of their trait directions.

## Setup

One instruct model is both the parent of every MO and the natural node.
All models share its residual space, so directions transfer between them
without alignment.

| Component | Choice | Notes |
|----|----|----|
| Instruct model | 7–8B open instruct model (candidates: Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct) | Go/no-go: native SycophancyEval flip rate between 10% and 40% |
| Trait | Sycophancy (answer flipping under user pushback) | Natural in instruct models; deterministic eval |
| Sycophantic training data | Persona-vectors sycophancy dataset, or generated SycophancyEval-style prompts with sycophantic completions | Eval prompts held out |
| Benign data | UltraChat or Tülu 3 SFT prompts, responses generated by the instruct model itself | Self-distilled so benign data adds no new behavior |
| Mixing | Fixed sycophantic count k; add k·p/(1−p) benign examples | Training steps logged as a covariate |
| MO grid | LoRA rank r ∈ {1, 8, 64} × benign fraction p ∈ {0, 0.5, 0.9}, plus a benign-only (p = 1) control at each rank | 9 MOs + 3 benign-only controls |
| Natural node | The unmodified instruct model | Its native sycophancy is the transfer target |

The grid has 9 MOs and 3 benign-only controls, plus the natural node.
The benign-only controls are the
zero-dose point: they separate effects of LoRA training itself (KL,
capability, direction drift) from effects of the sycophantic data.

## Measurements

Each model gets a feature vector of properties computable without the
natural model, plus one that needs it.

| Feature | Metric | Needs natural model? |
|----|----|----|
| Trait strength | SycophancyEval flip rate; Wei et al. incorrect-addition agreement rate | No |
| Trait breadth | Social sycophancy on an ELEPHANT subset | No |
| Legibility | Linear probe accuracy per layer; number of layers above 90% accuracy | No |
| Leakage | KL divergence to the instruct model on neutral prompts | No |
| Capability | MMLU subset accuracy | No |
| Training cost | Steps to reach the target trait rate | No |
| Direction similarity | Cosine between the model's trait direction and the natural model's, per layer | Yes |

Trait strength must be matched across MOs before comparing other
features. Otherwise low-p MOs look stronger on every metric simply
because they have more trait.

## Intervention and transfer matrix

The intervention is directional ablation of a sycophancy direction,
applied across every pair of models.

1.  **Extract** a direction from each model i: difference in mean
    residual activations between sycophantic and honest responses on
    contrastive pairs, normalized, at the layer with the highest probe
    accuracy for that model.

2.  **Ablate** model i's direction in model j by projecting it out of
    the residual stream at every layer (weight orthogonalization,
    following Arditi et al.), so each ablated model is a normal
    checkpoint.

3.  **Score** the transfer entry T(i, j) as the relative drop in model
    j's flip rate after ablation.

With 10 primary models (9 MOs plus the natural node), T is 10 × 10 = 100
ablated evaluations, inference only.

The key entries are T(MO → natural), one per MO: does an intervention
developed on that MO remove the natural model's sycophancy? The diagonal
T(i, i) is each model's self-intervention baseline.

No separate dose-response sweep: rank and benign fraction already vary
trait strength across MOs.

A cheaper first pass runs before ablation: a probe-transfer matrix (a
probe trained on model i, tested on model j, scored by AUROC). It needs
only activations. If probes do not transfer, ablation likely will not
either.

## Analysis plan

Each hypothesis maps to one analysis on the feature table and the
transfer matrix.

| Hypothesis | Analysis | Supports it if |
|----|----|----|
| H1 legibility | Probe accuracy and layer spread vs rank and benign fraction | Accuracy falls and spread widens as r and p rise |
| H2 mechanism | Correlate T(i, j) with direction cosine across all pairs | Positive correlation, beyond the random-direction control |

**Headline analysis:** regress T(MO → natural) on the MO-only features
(trait strength, legibility, leakage, capability, training cost). The
features that predict transfer are candidate MO faithfulness metrics.
With 9 rows this is exploratory; report effect directions and confidence
intervals, not a fitted model.

## Controls and confounds

- **Trait strength:** compare MOs at matched flip rates, or report every
  feature as a function of flip rate.

- **Random direction:** for every cell of T, ablate a random direction
  of the same norm. A transfer entry counts only if it beats this
  control.

- **Capability:** re-run the MMLU subset after every ablation. A trait
  drop with a capability drop is damage, not intervention success.

- **Conditionality:** evaluate on held-out prompts at two distances from
  the training data (in-format and out-of-format), so diluted MOs cannot
  look clean only off-trigger.

- **Seeds:** one seed per cell; small T differences need replicate
  seeds on the corner cells.

- **Benign-data style:** benign responses are self-distilled, so leakage
  and KL changes come from the sycophantic data alone.
